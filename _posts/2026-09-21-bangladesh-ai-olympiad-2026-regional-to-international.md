Bangladesh finished its 2026 AI olympiad season with three gold medals and four honourable mentions at the Asia-Pacific AI Olympiad, followed by three bronze medals at the International Olympiad in Artificial Intelligence in Astana. Behind those results was a national programme spanning regional and online rounds, a national selection stage and a four-day selection camp. Its registration records reached all eight divisions of Bangladesh.

The season tells two connected stories. One is the expansion of access to competitive AI beyond Dhaka. The other is the demanding transition from domestic selection to international machine-learning contests, where different tasks reward different strengths. The results show both a notable concentration of top-level success at APOAI and a broader need to develop the depth required for higher IOAI medals.

This account brings those stages together. Domestic participation is described through aggregate statistics. Individual profiles and scores are confined to Bangladesh's international representatives. Registration totals below count recorded registrations, while national call-list totals count invitations or contact-list entries. Neither should be read as an independently verified attendance total.

The Asia-Pacific event uses the official acronym **APOAI**; it is the event referred to as APAIO in some informal references. **IOAI**, the International Olympiad in Artificial Intelligence, is distinct from **IAIO**, the International Artificial Intelligence Olympiad. ## A regional programme with nationwide reach

The supplied season history describes in-person regional rounds in Chattogram, Saidpur and Dhaka, alongside online opportunities for Rajshahi, Khulna and other areas. The registration system reflects this mixture of physical and online access through six assigned routes. Rangpur is the administrative label associated with the Saidpur regional pathway. [2, 5]

A student's assigned route, preferred route and home division are different fields. For example, the master records contain 374 preferences for Dhaka, while the assigned Dhaka sheet contains 291 records. Similarly, 71 records explicitly preferred online participation, but 187 appear in the assigned E-Olympiad sheet. These differences should not be interpreted as attendance changes: they show that preferences and administrative routing were not interchangeable.

The assigned-route sheets contain one fewer Quiz record than the 968-record master. The master therefore supplies the season-wide demographic denominator, while the route table retains its own total of 967. Combining every sheet in the folder would inflate participation because many are copies, subsets or alternative views of the same registrations. [1, 2]

The regional result files also document selection decisions without needing to publish marks. The Rangpur coding selection list contains 19 entries, comprising eight labelled regional winners and eleven labelled selected. The E-Olympiad coding result file contains 25 entries: ten regional winners and fifteen selected for the national round. These lists overlap the national call list and include administrative route differences, so they must not be added to it as extra participants. No regional marks are reproduced here. [6]

The master contains 249 AI registrations, or 25.7% of all records, and 719 Quiz registrations, or 74.3%. The larger Quiz intake gave the programme a broad introductory audience, while the AI category formed the more specialised competitive track. The figures describe registration choices rather than demonstrated proficiency or eligibility decisions. [1]

### Gender distribution

Female students accounted for 180 registration records, or 18.6%, and male students for 788, or 81.4%. The female share was 15.3% in AI, with 38 records, and 19.7% in Quiz, with 142 records. Gender is reported as recorded, without inference from names. [1]

The gap is a useful baseline for future outreach and retention work. It does not, by itself, measure differences in ability, access to training or selection fairness. Those questions require matched participation and progression data rather than registration totals alone.

## The four-day selection camp

The organiser confirmed that the identical files `results.xlsx` and `results (1).csv` contain the results of the four-day selection camp. They represent one scoreboard with 22 entries, not two competitions. The tasks were **Fairytale**, **Remote Triage**, **Oculus Mirage** and **Olimpicos**. [4]

The camp formed the next assessment stage before international representation. Bangladesh ultimately entered eight students at APOAI and four at IOAI. Every member of those international delegations appears in the camp scoreboard, allowing the international teams to be connected to the domestic preparation stage without exposing other campers' identities.

The scoreboard alone does not explain the complete selection policy. It should not be used to claim that the first eight places automatically became the APOAI team, or that the first four automatically became the IOAI team. The actual representative lists differ from such a simple interpretation. Availability, eligibility, additional assessment or other selection considerations cannot be established from the scores alone.

The confirmed camp duration is four days. The website's earlier three-day schedule differs from that account, so exact camp dates are not asserted here. The methodological appendix records the discrepancy separately from the confirmed results.

The third IOAI took place in **Astana, Kazakhstan, from 2 to 8 August 2026**. The individual contest comprised two six-hour rounds, each with three tasks. Medals were determined by the individual contest; the separate Team Challenge and GAITE contest must not be merged into its standings. At-home tasks were educational preparation and did not contribute to the final individual score. [10, 11]

## Understanding the international scoreboard

IOAI scores are normalised task results, not ordinary school-test percentages. The official scoring rules relate a submission's performance to a baseline and a task-specific upper reference. Final evaluation uses the contest's test data. A low numerical total can therefore still represent medal-level performance in a difficult field. [11]

| IOAI recognition | Contestants | Share of 440 | Lowest listed score |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gold | 37 | 8.4% | 222.5052 |
| Silver | 73 | 16.6% | 104.6378 |
| Bronze | 110 | 25.0% | 42.8335 |
| Honourable mention | 62 | 14.1% | 7.8988 |
| No award listed | 158 | 35.9% | 0.0000 |

The honourable-mention range is not a fourth continuous total-score band. Some contestants with higher totals than honourable-mention recipients have no award listed. The accompanying CSVs therefore preserve the published award labels rather than recreating them from total-score thresholds. [12]

## What the season suggests for future preparation

The APOAI and IOAI results cannot be compared as if the totals were measured on the same scale. They involved different tasks, fields and scoring contexts. Araf and Labib won APOAI golds and IOAI bronzes; Priom moved from an APOAI honourable mention to an IOAI bronze. Those outcomes show how much the challenge can change between contests, rather than providing a simple numerical measure of improvement or decline.

For future preparation, the results support reviewing how students establish working solutions, test them on appropriate validation data and allocate time between tasks. Bangladesh's IOAI medallists each depended on a different set of productive tasks. Training can use that diversity to examine both individual strengths and tasks on which the delegation earned little. These are analytical recommendations, not claims about what the 2026 coaches did or did not teach.

The domestic records also point to a practical administrative improvement: maintain a stable participant identifier across registration, regional attendance, national attendance, camp participation and final team selection. That would allow future season reports to distinguish reach from attendance and to calculate progression by division, class and gender accurately.

The clearest achievements of 2026 are already well supported: a registration base spanning every division, a documented four-day assessment camp, eight APOAI representatives with three top-ten gold-medal finishes, and three IOAI bronze medallists from a four-person delegation. The next opportunity is to turn that breadth of interest and those demonstrated strengths into sustained depth across the international task set.
